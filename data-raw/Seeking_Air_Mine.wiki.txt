==Summary==
*The Seeking Air Mine is a single-target air {{H|Traps|trap}}. It consists of a spiky metallic bomb attached to three black balloons with white skulls painted onto them.
*While the [[Air Bomb]] is considered as the aerial counterpart of the [[Bomb]], the Seeking Air Mine is considered the aerial counterpart of the {{H|Spring Trap}}, both dealing huge damage to a single target and has the potential to instantly defeat a troop, though it has a larger trigger radius and lacks a stun. 
*This trap will target air units with at least 5 housing spaces (including [[Healer]]s, [[Siege Machine]]s, [[Heroes]] and [[Pets]]). The only air troops with fewer than 5 housing spaces are [[Minion]]s, [[Lava Pup]]s (and other Pup variations), [[Bat Spell/Bat|Bats]], and [[Sneezy/Booger|Booger]]s, none of which can trigger the Seeking Air Mine, or get affected by it.
**The [[Angry Jelly]] cannot be targeted by the Seeking Air Mine while it is still brainwashing a Hero.
**Since the Seeking Air Mine can only target a single unit, unlike the [[Giga Bomb]] and [[Spear Trap]] from [[Clan Capital]], the Seeking Air Mine only counts housing spaces of single troops, rather than summing up.
*Like all traps, once it is activated, the Seeking Air Mine must be re-armed in order to be a part of the next defense (with the exception of [[Clan Wars]] and in [[Legend League Tournaments|Legend League]], where all traps are active for every enemy attack). Doing so is free and occurs automatically once the player logs into the game.

==Strategies==
===Defensive Strategies===
*A good place for the Seeking Air Mine is next to an [[Air Defense]]. When the enemy destroys an Air Defense and then deploys powerful flying units in the vicinity, the Seeking Air Mine can cripple one quickly, if not destroying one.
*Its inability to be used against [[Minion]]s can work out in favor of the defender. This means that only larger [[Army|troops]], where the Seeking Air Mine has more utility in, such as [[Balloon]]s, can be hit.
*The player can place it near the outside of their base to quickly take down any Healers that are deployed. However, more skilled players can deploy Balloons to test for Seeking Air Mines and defuse them. Therefore, it might be more advisable to place them deeper in the base, such as behind a row of defenses.
*It is best used on [[Dragon]]s or any other powerful flying unit. One can even use this to easily take out {{H|Baby Dragon}}s as Baby Dragons are usually deployed without cover from air units.
*Placing it next to the [[Town Hall]] to stop lone Dragons from destroying it.
*It is an effective way to counter a [[Grand Warden]] in air mode. They can do devastating damage, or knock him out in one hit if he is lower-leveled. This can be used to either force the attacker to use the Eternal Tome early (at a point in the attack where it is not important), or if the attacker does not react in time, prevent the Eternal Tome from activating at all, reducing the risk of a defensive loss.
**The same applies to the [[Minion Prince]], although he can tank up to two Seeking Air Mines because of his higher health, given that he is at a higher level.
**For the [[Dragon Duke]] however, while alone, he can resist Seeking Air Mines, although he can be damaged like normal if nearby aerial units are close enough to him. Even then, because of his extremely high health, it may require multiple Seeking Air Mines to defeat him unless he is already heavily damaged. 
**If the player has the setting "Automatically use Hero abilities on KO" turned on, the Grand Warden will be able to endure a hit from the Seeking Air Mine regardless of the level provided his ability has not yet been used. If the Seeking Air Mine would defeat him, the Grand Warden will use his ability and survive with barely any health remaining.
*It can be used to take down a [[Battle Blimp]] that is being used to finish off the Town Hall after a ground or air attack, due to the speed of the Seeking Air Mine being faster. However, this may have adverse effects since the Battle Blimp may spawn the Clan Castle troops in an unfavorable location, such as the P.E.K.K.A right near a Wizard Tower or even the Town Hall itself.
*At higher Town Hall levels, Seeking Air Mines can greatly reduce the health of aerial [[Siege Machines]], or even destroy them early. The player can spread out Seeking Air Mines in their base, so that they can cover as many possible Siege Machine directions as possible.
*It is usually not recommended to group together multiple Seeking Air Mines since they will all target the same unit, effectively wasting them.
**However, at Town Hall 9, where [[Lava Hound]]s are used often with Balloons, double Seeking Air Mines can be placed near two Air Defenses, to greatly damage Lava Hounds and make them more vulnerable to getting taken out early.

===Offensive Strategies===
*The Balloon is the best choice against this trap, as it occupies only 5 housing space. It gains even more value if too many traps are set off against once since they will all just hit the Balloon for nothing.
**This is useful in some [[Single Player Campaign|Single Player]] maps where a level can contain hundreds of them as a single Balloon will trigger dozens of Seeking Air Mines and waste them all.
**In the [[Mashup Mania]] event, the player could also use the [[Ice Minion]], since it is more housing space-efficient.
***However, since an update, the Seeking Air Mine requires at least 5 housing space to trigger, which means that the Ice Minion can no longer trigger Seeking Air Mines.<sup>[what update?]</sup>
*Using the Grand Warden's Eternal Tome ability can negate the damage done by the Seeking Air Mine, but if wishing to do so, activate the ability quickly, otherwise, the troop will get heavily damaged, if not get taken out. Success on the attacker's may be advantageous as they will have negated a powerful trap, but usually, the Eternal Tome is better used to negate damage from more dangerous sources, such as a Town Hall's [[Giga Bomb]].

==Upgrade Differences==
*When initially constructed, the Seeking Air Mine is a spherical, explosive [[wikipedia:Naval mine#Contact mines|contact mine]] made of metal with spikes as triggers and a small red dot at the center, some of which parts are hidden under a trapdoor just like the [[Air Bomb]]. Inside the mine, there is an explosive material, most likely [[wikipedia:TNT|trinitrotoluene]], and produces red smoke as it flies to the target. Three black balloons with white skulls are attached to the mine via a metal handle. When triggered, the mine leaves a red trail and its red dot blinks repeatedly as it intercepts its target, and explodes with a red vertical cloud will appear once it hits, dealing massive damage.
*At level 3, the spikes on the Seeking Air Mine become bigger and turn golden, and the Seeking Air Mine can be seen more easily within its compartment as it opens up slightly.
*At level 5, the mine's red dot becomes bigger, and some spikes are replaced in favor of larger ones with metal reinforcements that hold them. When it follows its target, the dot lights up repeatedly.
*At level 7, the amount of spikes of the mine increases to five at the top, and are bigger in size, with each one resembling a cactus with a spike at the end. The mine itself changes into a grey base with a golden circle at the top. The balloons are now shinier, making them look like a 3D model, in addition to the skulls being slightly changed.

==Trivia==
*The Seeking Air Mine appears to come out of its compartment and then hits an air troop, causing a small, red vertical flash.
*The Seeking Air Mine is heavier than an [[Air Bomb]], as it needs three balloons to carry it, which is most likely that the spiky metal ball has more weight than a wooden barrel.
*The Seeking Air Mine shares its initials with the Surface-to-Air Missile. Both are powerful, grounded explosives that devastate singular aerial units but are ineffective when enemies come in larger numbers, and are not used against weak aerial threats.
*After hitting an aerial unit, the bomb the balloons hold disappears, leaving deflated black balloons instead of an exploded one when triggered and viewed after a battle or when upgrading the trap. The same applies to the Air Bomb, but the colors are red.
*The Seeking Air Mine's description is a reference to the musical "It's a Bird, It's a Plane, It's Superman".

===Comparisons===
*The Seeking Air Mine is the only trap that can knock out the [[Grand Warden]] set to air mode in one hit, provided that the Seeking Air Mine is max-leveled and the Grand Warden is not equipped with Equipment that increases his max health.
*The Seeking Air Mine has the highest damage out of all the traps.
*The Seeking Air Mine, the Spring Trap, and the [[Skeleton Trap]] are the only traps that do not do splash damage.
